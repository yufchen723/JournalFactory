# Stage 3 · Story and structure

1. **One sentence.** What the reader will believe after the paper that they did not before. If it needs "and", there are two papers.
2. **Claims ladder.** 5–7 claims, each answering the question the previous one raises. One claim = one main figure. Under each: evidence in hand, evidence missing, the reviewer's first objection.
3. **Figure 1 is the paper in small**: design, cohort, technology, and the first proof that the data are good.
4. **Last figure is function.** In silico prediction alone is a hypothesis; pair it with a perturbation or state it as a limitation.
5. **Supplemental figures mirror main figures** (Cell: Figure S*n* tied to Figure *n*; Nature: Extended Data). They carry controls, per-sample views, robustness, and negative results that bound a claim.
6. **Panel table per figure**: letter · what it shows · status (four states) · data path · to do. Wireframe on the journal's column grid.
7. **Text logic before figures are drawn**: title options, summary, highlights, four-paragraph introduction (problem · what is known · gap · what we did), results headings as declarative sentences, discussion (what changes · relation to prior work · limits · next).
8. Write it as a journal-plan report (omics-report-studio), so the user can ask and edit per panel. Colour by status; never present a planned panel as done.
9. Strength check per figure: strong / good / exploratory / weak / not started, with the reason.
