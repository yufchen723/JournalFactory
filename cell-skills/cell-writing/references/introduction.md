# Introduction — drafting procedure

Adapted in structure from nature-skills (nature-writing/references/introduction.md), Apache-2.0; Cell rules from cell-shared/journal-formats/cell.md.

The shared contract (funnel, gap forms, closing statement, alignment table, audit list) is in
`../../cell-shared/core/cell-introduction.md`. Load it with this file. This file is the procedure.

## Cell rules (source: cell.md §3, §9)

- Succinct; pertinent background only.
- Ends with a brief statement of what was achieved.
- No subheadings.
- Counts toward the main-text limit of under 7,000 words (cell.md §2).
- References numbered in order of first citation, as superscripts.
- "novel" and "first" discouraged; US spelling; abbreviations defined at first use; no footnotes.

Not specified by Cell (see cell.md): a word or paragraph count.

## Inputs needed

| Input | Used for |
|---|---|
| The question the first Results subsections answer | the gap sentence |
| Central claim and two supports | the closing statement |
| What prior work established, and where it stops | paragraphs 2–3 |
| The obstacle that prevented an answer, if any | optional paragraph 4 |
| Ledger terms | every definition made here |

Write the Introduction after Results. Its gap must be the one the Results close.

## Procedure (craft guidance, not a journal rule)

1. **Write the gap sentence.** "Whether / how / which ______ is unknown." Test: the first
   Results subheading begins to answer it.
2. **Write the closing statement** (2–4 sentences): finding, mechanism, consequence.
   "Here, we show that …". No figure-by-figure tour.
3. **Work backward.** List the facts a reader needs to understand the gap sentence. Each fact
   becomes one or two sentences in paragraph 2 or 3. Facts not on the list are cut.
4. **Write the opening paragraph last.** State the biological problem at the level of a
   biologist from another field. Reach the specific phenomenon by its final sentence.
5. **Remove subheadings and bold run-in heads.** Order comes from topic sentences.
6. **Count and trim** to the budget (500–800 words is a working target, not a Cell number).
7. **Run the alignment table** of `cell-introduction.md` §6.

## Paragraph skeleton

```
P1  [Problem]  One biological problem; why it matters; the specific phenomenon.
P2  [Known]    What is established about the phenomenon. Grouped by finding, not by paper.
P3  [Gap]      Where knowledge stops or conflicts. Ends with the exact unknown.
P4  [Obstacle] (optional) Why it was unanswered; what now makes it answerable.
P5  [Achieved] "Here, we show that ..." finding; mechanism; consequence.
```

## Topic sentences that carry the structure

Without subheadings, the first sentence of each paragraph must tell the reader where they are.

| Paragraph | Weak opening | Strong opening |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | "The intestine is a complex organ." | "Epithelial tissues must replace lost cells without exhausting their stem cells." |
| P2 | "Many studies have investigated stem cells." | "After injury, quiescent stem cells enter the cell cycle in numbers that scale with damage." |
| P3 | "However, much remains unknown." | "Niche signals permit activation but do not vary with the amount of damage." |
| P5 | "In this study, we investigated factor X." | "Here, we show that factor X, released by dying cells, sets how many stem cells divide." |

## Literature use

- Cite to build the gap: what was shown, in which system, and what it leaves open.
- One citation group per fact. Do not narrate papers ("Smith et al. showed … Then Lee et al. …").
- Cite the work closest to the claim and say how the question differs. Leaving it out invites
  the reviewer to supply it.
- Unpublished data and personal communications are cited in the text only, not in the
  reference list (cell.md §9). Preprints with a DOI may be listed (cell.md §9).

## Common faults

| Fault | Fix |
|---|---|
| Background longer than the paper needs | keep only facts used by the gap or by Results |
| Gap is "poorly understood" | name what is unknown |
| Closing statement lists experiments | state the finding; methods go in one phrase |
| Closing claim stronger than the Summary or the data | match the verb to the evidence |
| Result details and numbers in the Introduction | move to Results |
| Discussion material (implications, models) in the Introduction | one sentence of consequence at most |
| Abbreviations defined here and never used again | spell out |
| "novel", "for the first time" | delete |

## Deliverable

The Introduction, its word count, the gap sentence quoted on its own line, and the alignment
table (gap ↔ first Results subsection; closing claim ↔ Summary ↔ Discussion opening). List
every placeholder citation as `[ref: what is needed]`; never invent a reference.
