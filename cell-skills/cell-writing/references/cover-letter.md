# Cover letter

Adapted in structure from nature-skills (nature-writing/references/submission-package.md), Apache-2.0; Cell rules from cell-shared/journal-formats/cell.md.

## What Cell states (source: cell.md)

| Statement | Source |
|---|---|
| At revision, a cover letter and a point-by-point response are required | cell.md §1 |
| A research article reports "conceptual advances of unusual significance regarding a biological question of wide interest" | cell.md §2 |
| A Resource reports a technical advance with proof of principle, or a major database with "provocative new biological insights" | cell.md §2 |
| A Theory article requires testable predictions | cell.md §2 |
| "novel" and "first" are discouraged in the manuscript | cell.md §3 |
| Exactly one lead contact, who must be a corresponding author | cell.md §3 |
| Declaration of interests must match the form | cell.md §3 |
| Generative-AI use in preparing the manuscript must be declared | cell.md §3 |

Not specified by Cell (see cell.md): whether a cover letter is required at initial submission;
its length; its required content; reviewer suggestions or exclusions; statements about related
manuscripts, prior presubmission inquiries or prior reviews; to whom it is addressed.
The initial-submission page was not among the pages read. Check the submission system before
sending.

Everything below is craft guidance (not a journal rule).

## Purpose

The letter tells the editor, in under a page, what biological question the paper answers, what
the answer is, and why readers outside the subfield should care. It does not summarize every
figure, and it does not replace the Summary.

## Structure (about 300–450 words)

| Paragraph | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title, article type, one-sentence finding | 2–3 sentences |
| 2 | The question and why it was open | 3–4 sentences |
| 3 | What the paper shows: finding, decisive evidence, mechanism | 4–5 sentences |
| 4 | Why it matters broadly; who will use it | 2–4 sentences |
| 5 | Closest prior or concurrent work and how this paper differs | 2–3 sentences |
| 6 | Administrative statements | as needed |

Paragraphs 2–4 map onto Cell's own criterion (cell.md §2): the biological question of wide
interest, the conceptual advance, its significance. Use plain statements; the editor will judge
the adjectives.

## Sentence patterns

| Job | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Opening | "We submit '[title]' for consideration as a [research article / Resource / Theory article] in Cell." |
| Finding | "We show that [actor] [does what] in [system], which explains [biological question]." |
| Open question | "It has been unclear whether / how ______, because ______." |
| Evidence | "[Loss of A] prevents [B]; [A alone] is sufficient for [B]; [A] acts through [C]." |
| Breadth | "The result bears on [field 1] and [field 2] because ______." |
| Prior work | "[Reference] showed ______; it did not address ______, which we resolve by ______." |

## What to leave out

- "novel", "first", "unprecedented", "paradigm-shifting" (Cell discourages the first two in the
  manuscript; the same restraint applies here).
- A figure-by-figure tour.
- Claims stronger than the manuscript makes. The letter uses the Summary's verbs.
- Impact predictions ("will be highly cited").
- Any statement about journal policy that is not in cell.md.

## Administrative statements: include only what is true and asked for

Confirm each against the submission system; cell.md does not list them.

- The work is original and not under consideration elsewhere.
- All authors approved the submission.
- Related manuscripts by the authors that are under review or in press, if any.
- Competing interests, matching the manuscript's Declaration of interests (cell.md §3).
- The lead contact (one corresponding author; cell.md §3).
- Reviewer suggestions or exclusions, with a factual reason, if the system asks for them.

## Revision cover letter

Cell requires a cover letter with the point-by-point response at revision (cell.md §1). Its
content is not specified by Cell (see cell.md). Working form:

1. Thank the editor and reviewers in one sentence.
2. State the main changes in three to five bullets: new experiments, changed conclusions,
   restructured figures.
3. State anything requested that was not done, and why.
4. Confirm the format changes required at revision: component order (cell.md §3), STAR Methods
   (cell.md §8), legends (cell.md §4), main text under 7,000 words and at most 7 display items
   (cell.md §2).
5. Leave the detailed answers to the point-by-point response.

## Consistency checks

| Item in the letter | Must equal |
|---|---|
| Title | the manuscript title, character for character |
| Finding sentence | the Summary's central claim, same verb |
| Article type | the type whose limits were applied (cell.md §2) |
| Figure and supplemental numbers mentioned | the current manuscript |
| Terms | the Terminology Ledger |

## Skeleton

```
[Date]

Dear Editor,

We submit "[Title]" for consideration as a [article type] in Cell.

[Question: 3–4 sentences. What is unknown and why it matters to biologists broadly.]

[Answer: 4–5 sentences. Finding; decisive evidence; mechanism; model system.]

[Significance: 2–4 sentences. What changes; which fields are affected.]

[Context: 2–3 sentences. Closest prior or concurrent work; the difference.]

[Administrative statements that apply.]

Sincerely,
[Corresponding author], on behalf of all authors
[Affiliation; email]
```

## Deliverable

The letter, its word count, and a list of every statement that needs the author's confirmation
(related manuscripts, competing interests, reviewer names). Never invent an editor's name, a
reviewer, or a related manuscript.
