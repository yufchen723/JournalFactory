# Default stance (cell-writing)

Adapted in structure from nature-skills (nature-writing/static/core/stance.md), Apache-2.0; Cell rules from cell-shared/journal-formats/cell.md.

## 1. Two kinds of statement, always labeled

| Kind | How to write it | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Cell rule | "Cell rule (source: cell.md §N)" | `../../../cell-shared/journal-formats/cell.md` only |
| Craft guidance | "craft guidance (not a journal rule)" | this skill; the author may overrule it |

- A number, section name or format that is not in cell.md is not a Cell rule. Write
  "not specified by Cell (see cell.md)".
- Never fill a gap with a Nature rule, a rule from another Cell Press journal, or memory.
- cell.md lists pages that were not read (STAR Methods author guide PDF, journal-policies page,
  final file requirements PDF, video guidelines). When a task depends on one, say so and ask the
  author to open the page.

## 2. Author evidence comes first

- Do not invent results, mechanisms, sample sizes, statistics, reagents, identifiers, accession
  numbers, references or limitations.
- Write the argument before the sentences.
- Use placeholders in square brackets for missing facts and list them under
  `Assumptions or missing inputs`.
- Ask only when an unresolved choice changes the central claim, the meaning of the evidence or
  the requested output. Otherwise proceed and state the assumption.

## 3. Stage awareness (Cell rule, cell.md §1)

| Stage | Consequence for text |
|---|---|
| Initial submission | free format; STAR Methods format not required; do not fail a draft on format rules, report them as "needed later" |
| Revision | components and order of cell.md §3; legends per §4; STAR Methods required |
| Final files | highlights and eTOC blurb in one Word file separate from the manuscript (§6) |

The article-type limits in cell.md §2 (words, display items) are properties of the article type.
Hold to them at every stage (craft guidance: cell.md does not say whether editors enforce them
at first submission).

## 4. Claim discipline

- One central claim per paper; every part serves it.
- Cell asks for "conceptual advances of unusual significance regarding a biological question of
  wide interest" (cell.md §2). State the biological question and the advance in plain terms; do
  not assert significance with adjectives.
- Cell discourages "novel" and "first" (cell.md §3). Remove them. Also remove "unprecedented",
  "for the first time", "paradigm shift" (craft guidance).
- Match verbs to evidence (craft guidance):

| Evidence | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Direct perturbation with controls | show, demonstrate, establish |
| Loss of function | is required for |
| Gain of function | is sufficient to |
| Correlation, association, inference from omics | is associated with, correlates with, suggests |
| Untested extension | may, could |

- The same claim keeps the same verb in the Summary, Introduction close, Results subheading,
  figure title, Discussion, Highlights and eTOC blurb.

## 5. Intake for the requested scope

Identify before writing:

- **part(s)** and **stage** (manifest axes), and the **article type** (cell.md §2)
- **central claim**: what the paper shows, in one sentence
- **evidence**: the main figures and what each one establishes
- **model system**: species, tissue or cell type, key perturbations
- **boundary**: where the claim stops (feeds "Limitations of the study")
- **terminology**: build the ledger on first contact (`../../../cell-shared/core/terminology-ledger.md`)

A missing item does not stop all drafting. Draft the independent passages and mark the gaps.
For a local edit, keep the established argument and terms; do not restart the intake.

## 6. House style of this skill's output

- US spelling; abbreviations defined at first use; genes italic, proteins roman; no footnotes
  (Cell rules, cell.md §3).
- Short sentences. One message per paragraph.
- Return prose first, then notes: assumptions, missing inputs, counts against limits, and the
  checker's FAIL/WARN lines.
