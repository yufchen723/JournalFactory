# Tone and stance

*Adapted in structure from nature-skills (nature-response/references/tone-and-stance.md), Apache-2.0; Cell rules from cell-shared/journal-formats/cell.md.*

Craft guidance (not a journal rule). cell.md says nothing about the tone of a response.

## 1. The voice

- A colleague reporting what was done. Calm, specific, brief.
- The reader is an expert who is short of time and wants to verify, not to be persuaded.
- First sentence: the answer. Then the evidence. Then the location.

## 2. Openers that work

| Situation | Opener |
|---|---|
| Agree, done | "We agree. We have now [action] ([location])." |
| Agree in part | "We agree that [part]. For [other part], [reason]; we have therefore [action]." |
| New data | "We performed this experiment. [Result with numbers] (Figure NX)." |
| Clarify | "We have clarified this. The text now reads: '[quote]'." |
| Already there but hard to find | "This information was in [old location] but was not easy to find. We have moved it to [new location] and now state it in the main text." |
| Narrow the claim | "We agree that the data do not establish [strong claim]. We now state [narrow claim] in the title, Summary and Results, and discuss the limit in 'Limitations of the study'." |
| Disagree | "We respectfully disagree, for the following reason. [Evidence.] To avoid the ambiguity that led to this concern, we have [change]." |
| Cannot do | "We were not able to perform this experiment because [concrete constraint]. To address the underlying concern, we [substitute]. We state this limit in [location]." |

## 3. Phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use |
|---|---|---|
| "We thank the reviewer for this insightful comment" on every item | Ritual; adds length | One specific thanks in the opening; thanks at an item only when the comment improved the paper, saying how |
| "The reviewer misunderstood" / "failed to notice" | Blames the reader | "We did not explain this clearly. We have revised…" |
| "As clearly stated in the manuscript" | Same | Give the location; improve its visibility |
| "Obviously", "of course", "it is well known" | Dismissive | Cite the evidence |
| "We believe" as the whole argument | Assertion | Data or citation |
| "We will…" / "In future work…" | A promise is not a revision | Do it, or state the limit |
| "Beyond the scope" with no reason | Reads as refusal | Say why, and what was done instead |
| "Novel", "first" in new text | **Cell rule** (cell.md §3): discouraged | State the finding |
| "Unfortunately" before every limit | Apologetic noise | State the constraint |
| Long history of how the project evolved | Not the reviewer's question | Cut |

## 4. Disagreement

Disagree when the request rests on a factual error, would not test the claim, or is disproportionate to its value. Then:

1. Restate the concern fairly in one clause.
2. Give the reason: data in the paper, a published result (verified), or a design argument.
3. Concede what is right in the concern.
4. Change something anyway: a sentence that prevents the misreading, a stated limit, a control shown.
5. Leave the judgment to the editor without saying so in the response; raise it in the cover letter if it matters.

Never disagree by tone. One "respectfully" per item is enough.

## 5. When the reviewer missed something

Treat it as the manuscript's failure to make it visible.

```text
The control the reviewer asks for was shown in the former Figure S3B. We agree it is
important enough for the main figure and have moved it to Figure 2E. The Results now
state the outcome directly.
```

## 6. Length and density

- S4 items: "Corrected." or one sentence.
- S3: two to four sentences.
- S1–S2: as long as the evidence needs; put numbers in the text, not adjectives.
- If a response runs past half a page, check that the first sentence still answers the comment.

## 7. Consistency of terms

- Same names for genes, cell types, groups and figures as in the manuscript.
- Gene symbols italic, proteins roman (**Cell rule**, cell.md §3).
- US spelling in new manuscript text (**Cell rule**, cell.md §3). Quotes of the reviewer stay as written.

## 8. Cover-letter tone

- Factual and brief. The editor wants to know: what changed, what did not and why, whether conditions were met.
- No claims about what reviewers "will" think.
- No pressure about competing work unless a concrete, verifiable fact matters for timing.
