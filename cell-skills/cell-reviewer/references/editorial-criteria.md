# Editorial criteria

## 1. What Cell says it selects for — Cell rule (cell.md §2)

These are the only criteria that may be quoted as Cell's.

| Article type | Criterion as recorded in cell.md §2 |
|---|---|
| Research article | "conceptual advances of unusual significance regarding a biological question of wide interest" |
| Resource | a technical advance with proof of principle, or a major database with "provocative new biological insights" |
| Theory | testable predictions required |
| Short article | no separate criterion recorded; limits are ≤ 4,000 words and ≤ 4 display items |
| Matters Arising | no separate criterion recorded; same limits as a research article; no revision round |

Related rules that shape how the advance is presented:

- Title "conveys the broad conceptual significance" (cell.md §3).
- Summary covers background/purpose, principal results with approach and main conclusions, and broader significance, in ≤ 150 words (cell.md §3).
- Cell discourages "novel" and "first": "overused … difficult to verify" (cell.md §3).
- The Discussion includes "Limitations of the study", specific to the paper (cell.md §3).

Nothing else about selection is in cell.md. For acceptance rates, scope statements or editor preferences: not specified in cell.md — check cell.com.

## 2. Reading the criteria — inference (not Cell policy)

Everything below is an interpretation to help self-review.

### Research article: unpack the sentence

| Phrase | Question to ask of the draft |
|---|---|
| "conceptual advances" | Does the paper change how a process is understood, or does it add an instance to a known idea? Can the advance be said in one sentence without a method name? |
| "of unusual significance" | What would a reader now do or believe differently? Is the effect central or marginal to the biology? |
| "a biological question" | Is there a question, stated in the Introduction, that the Results answer? Or is the paper organised around a dataset? |
| "of wide interest" | Who outside the subfield would read it, and why? Name two communities. |

### Resource

| Route | Question |
|---|---|
| Technical advance with proof of principle | What can be done now that could not be done before? Is the benchmark against existing methods fair and quantitative? Does the proof of principle show a biological result, not only that the method runs? |
| Major database with "provocative new biological insights" | Is the dataset large or unique enough to be reused? Which specific insight is provocative, and is it supported beyond description? Is the data access route real (see `cell-data`)? |

### Choosing between research article and Resource

- A paper whose main value is a method or dataset, with findings that are mostly descriptive, reads as a Resource.
- A paper that answers a mechanistic question, with the method in a supporting role, reads as a research article.
- A paper that claims both often satisfies neither. A referee will ask which claim the authors stand on.

### Theory

- The predictions must be testable: name the experiment that would falsify each one.

## 3. Signals that the advance is not yet stated — inference

- The Summary lists what was done, not what was found.
- Results subheadings name analyses, not findings. cell.md §3 asks for subheadings that state the finding.
- The central claim needs "novel", "first" or "comprehensive" to sound important.
- The last paragraph of the Introduction cannot say in two sentences what was achieved.
- The Highlights (≤ 4, each ≤ 85 characters; cell.md §6) cannot be written without abbreviations or method names.
- The graphical abstract cannot be drawn as one process (cell.md §6 asks for one process or one point).

## 4. How to report this section in a review

```text
Advance as claimed:        [one sentence, the authors' claim]
Advance as supported:      [one sentence, what the data establish]
Gap:                       [what separates the two]
Article-type fit (inference): research article / Resource / unclear, and why
Readership (inference):    [communities that would care]
```

Always label the fit and readership lines as inference.
