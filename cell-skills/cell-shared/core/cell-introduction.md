# The Cell Introduction

Adapted in structure from nature-skills (nature-shared/core/nature-introduction.md), Apache-2.0; Cell rules from cell-shared/journal-formats/cell.md.

Use this file to draft, restructure or audit the Introduction of a Cell research article.

## 1. Cell rules (source: cell.md §3)

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | "succinct". No word limit of its own; it counts toward the main-text total of under 7,000 words (cell.md §2) |
| Content | pertinent background |
| Ending | a brief statement of what was achieved |
| Subheadings | **none** |
| Citations | numbered in order of first citation, superscript (cell.md §9) |
| Novelty words | "novel" and "first" discouraged |
| Style | US spelling; abbreviations minimal and defined at first use; no footnotes |

Not specified by Cell (see cell.md): a paragraph count, a word target for the Introduction,
whether the closing statement may preview individual results.

Everything below is craft guidance (not a journal rule).

## 2. The funnel

Build a narrowing argument, not a topic overview:

`broad biological problem → the specific phenomenon → what is established → the tension or missing link → the exact unknown → the question → what this study achieved`

Typical shape: four to five paragraphs, about 500–800 words. Fewer words here leave more for Results.

| Paragraph | Job | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The biological problem and why it matters to biologists outside the subfield | A reader from another field can say why the problem is hard |
| 2 | What is known about the specific phenomenon | Every cited fact is needed by paragraph 3 or by Results |
| 3 | The exact gap | Completes: "It remains unclear whether / how / which ______" |
| 4 (optional) | Why the gap could not be closed before, and what makes it tractable now | Names a specific obstacle, not "technical limitations" |
| Last | What was achieved | Two to four sentences; the central finding at full strength |

Move fast. By the end of paragraph 1 the reader should see the specific phenomenon, not only the
field.

## 3. State an exact knowledge gap

A gap is something unknown, disputed or untested. Good forms:

- which factor or cell type causes an observed effect
- whether a relationship is causal or correlative
- how a signal is sensed, transmitted or limited
- why two credible sets of results disagree
- under which conditions a known mechanism fails

Weak forms and their repair:

| Weak | Problem | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| "X remains poorly understood" | Does not say what is unknown | "Whether X acts on stem cells directly or through the niche is unknown" |
| "No study has examined X in Y" | Absence of a study is not a scientific unknown | Say what examining X in Y would resolve |
| "Existing approaches have limitations" | No named limitation | Name the measurement that could not be made and what it hides |
| A gap the Results do not close | Promise and delivery differ | Narrow the gap to what the main figures answer |

Use the literature to build the gap. Group prior work by what it established, then show where
those findings stop or conflict. A list of papers, one sentence each, is not a gap.

## 4. The closing statement

Cell asks the Introduction to end with a brief statement of what was achieved (cell.md §3).

- State the finding, not the plan: "Here, we show that factor X …", not "Here, we investigated …".
- Approach and model system go in one subordinate phrase.
- One central claim, at most two supporting statements, one sentence of consequence.
- Do not walk through the figures ("First, we … Next, we … Finally, we …").
- Do not write "novel" or "for the first time" (Cell discourages both).

Example (invented):

> Here, we show that factor X, released by dying epithelial cells, sets how many intestinal
> stem cells divide after injury. Factor X binds receptor Y on quiescent stem cells and
> activates pathway Z in proportion to the number of dying cells. This dose-dependent signal
> explains how repair scales with damage without exhausting the stem cell pool.

## 5. No subheadings: how to keep order without them

- One job per paragraph (table in section 2). The first sentence of each paragraph states that job.
- Link paragraphs by content: the last sentence of one sets up the first sentence of the next.
- If the draft seems to need subheadings, it is too long or carries two stories. Cut background
  that neither narrows the problem nor is used in Results.

## 6. Alignment with Results and Discussion

Build this table before finalizing; every row must be filled.

| Introduction element | Must match |
|---|---|
| The exact unknown (paragraph 3) | The question answered by the first one or two Results subsections |
| Each term defined here | The same term in Results subheadings and figure titles (Terminology Ledger) |
| The closing claim | The Summary's main claim and the first Discussion paragraph, at the same strength |
| Each background fact | Used later in Results or Discussion; otherwise delete |
| Any limit of prior approaches named here | Addressed by the approach in Results, or listed in "Limitations of the study" |

If the Results answer a different question from the one the Introduction asks, rewrite the
Introduction. Do not bend the Results.

## 7. Audit list

1. No subheadings (Cell rule).
2. Ends with what was achieved, as a finding (Cell rule).
3. The gap sentence can be quoted alone and still makes sense.
4. Background facts are all used downstream.
5. Closing claim, Summary claim and Discussion opening agree in content and strength.
6. No "novel"/"first"; abbreviations defined at first use; US spelling (Cell rules).
7. Citations are numbered in order of first appearance (Cell rule, cell.md §9).
